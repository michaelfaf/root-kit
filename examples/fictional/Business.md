## 1. Business model

Ridgeline sells annual grounds-maintenance contracts, then upsells enhancement work — irrigation rebuilds, planting, hardscape repair — into the same accounts. 80% recurring, 20% enhancement, and the latter depends on the former [Dana, 2026-07-28].

**All labor is W-2 — no subcontracted mowing, ever.** Structural, not a phase: wages sit in cost of goods rather than a broker markup, and a bad crew is Ridgeline's problem the same day — the reason property managers renew instead of shopping the incumbent. Snow and ice (November–March) isn't a side business — it's why the model works, keeping crew leads employed through winter instead of an October layoff.

**Current scope:** grounds-maintenance contracts, snow/ice contracts, per-job enhancement projects. **Coming:** a Greeley branch, go/no-go by 2026-10-15, blocked on the Q3 margin audit [Dana, 2026-07-28]. **Out of scope:** residential mowing and design-build (both discontinued, §9); subcontracted labor of any kind.

## 3. Pricing & margin mechanics

Grounds contracts: fixed monthly fee across 12 months, never per visit. Snow and ice: fixed seasonal fee, never per-push. Enhancement projects: bid per job, 15% minimum contribution margin. A standard rate card is the starting point; per-account rates drift as contracts renew — a floor, not a price list [Dana, 2026-06-02].

> **Live numbers:** each account's actual rate lives in Aspire. No canonical price list here.

**Margin mechanics:** crew wages are COGS; office staff and Dana's own comp are overhead — don't blur the two. Benchmark only against other W-2, self-performing operators, never brokers or subs showing 55%+ gross. History: 33% gross in 2024, ~38% in 2025 [bookkeeper, 2026 year-end close] — renewal pricing catching up to real labor cost, not volume. The lever that moves margin most: the 15% enhancement floor and snow retention.

## 4. Value proposition & competition

**Throughline:** *You get one person who knows your property, every year, not whoever the incumbent sends this week.* One named account manager per property · W-2 crews with quality control Ridgeline actually controls · fixed seasonal pricing both sides of the year.

**Positioning:** *"We're the vendor you stop thinking about, because nothing ever goes wrong long enough to notice us."* **Not:** the cheapest bid — Ridgeline doesn't chase boards that only read the bottom line.

**Competitive landscape:** the incumbent at each property is the real competitor on every renewal, not a named company. Front Range Property Services competes on price at the B/C tier; Cache La Poudre Landscape sits closer to Ridgeline's positioning. **Known limit:** loses on straight price where the board only reads the number — lost a 120-unit HOA in 2025 to a bid 18% under [Dana, 2026-07-28]. Not a mistake to fix; a signal to stop chasing that kind of board.

## 8. Rules that must not be gotten wrong

- **Snow is quoted seasonal-fixed, never per-event.** A light winter is pure profit on a fee already collected. A heavy winter is the cost of a client who stays ten years. Anyone benchmarking Ridgeline against a per-push snow operator is comparing the wrong business entirely.
- **Crew wages are cost of goods; office staff and Dana's comp are overhead.** Ridgeline's gross margin is only a valid comparison against other W-2, self-performing operators — never against broker or subcontractor models, which post 55%+ gross on a completely different cost structure.
- **No subcontracted mowing crews, ever.** Not a cost decision up for revisiting when margin gets tight — it's the quality-control mechanism the entire recurring-contract model depends on. Proposing subcontracted labor to fix a margin problem misunderstands what's actually being sold.

## 9. Discontinued / pivots

**Residential mowing routes — discontinued March 2024.** Route density never worked in a market this spread out, and every stop competed for crew hours against contracts that pay year-round through snow. Not coming back.

**Design-build landscape architecture division — opened 2023, shut down 2024.** Wrong skillset for a maintenance crew, and it pulled Dana's attention off renewals during a season that needed it. Permanent.
