## 1. Business model

Ridgeline sells annual grounds-maintenance contracts, then upsells one-off enhancement work — irrigation rebuilds, planting, hardscape repair — into the same accounts. 80% recurring contract base, 20% enhancement work, and the latter depends on the former existing first [Dana, 2026-07-28].

**All labor is W-2, deliberately — no subcontracted mowing crews, ever.** Structural, not a phase to grow out of: crew wages sit in cost of goods rather than a broker markup, growth means training crew leads rather than vetting subs, and a bad crew is Ridgeline's problem the same day — the reason property managers renew instead of shopping the incumbent.

Snow and ice (November–March) isn't a side business — it's why the W-2 model works, keeping crew leads employed through winter instead of a seasonal layoff every October.

### What you sell

**Current scope:** annual grounds-maintenance contracts (mow, trim, bed maintenance, irrigation checks, seasonal cleanup); seasonal snow and ice contracts; enhancement projects bid per job.

**Coming:** a Greeley branch — go/no-go by 2026-10-15, blocked on the Q3 Anchor-account margin audit clearing first [Dana, 2026-07-28].

**Out of scope:** residential mowing and design-build (both discontinued, §9); anything requiring subcontracted labor.

## 3. Pricing & margin mechanics

### How you charge

- Annual grounds contracts: fixed monthly fee spread evenly across 12 months — never priced per visit.
- Snow and ice: fixed seasonal fee, never per-push, never per-event.
- Enhancement projects: bid per job, 15% minimum contribution margin before a bid goes out.
- A standard rate card is the starting point for a new bid; per-account rates drift from it as contracts renew — the card is a floor, not a price list [Dana, 2026-06-02].

> **Live numbers:** each account's actual monthly rate lives in Aspire. No canonical price list here — a printed number would be wrong within a quarter.

### Margin mechanics

- **Crew wages are cost of goods sold; office staff and Dana's own comp are overhead.** Don't blur the two in any margin read.
- **Benchmark only against other W-2, self-performing grounds operators** — never against broker or subcontractor models showing 55%+ gross. Different business wearing the same industry code.
- **Margin history:** 33% gross in 2024, ~38% in 2025 [bookkeeper, 2026 year-end close] — renewal pricing catching up to real labor cost, not volume.
- **The lever that moves margin most:** the 15% enhancement-project floor and snow retention — a mild winter is pure profit on a fee already collected; a heavy one is the cost of keeping a ten-year client, not a loss.

## 4. Value proposition & competition

**Throughline:** *You get one person who knows your property, every year, not whoever the incumbent sends this week.*

**The uniques:**
1. One named account manager per property, not a rotating dispatcher.
2. W-2 crews with quality control Ridgeline actually controls.
3. Fixed seasonal pricing both sides of the year — no surprise per-push snow bills.

**Positioning:** *"We're the vendor you stop thinking about, because nothing ever goes wrong long enough to notice us."*

**What the claim is NOT:** not the cheapest bid in the room, and doesn't chase boards that only read the bottom line — a fight the model isn't built to win.

### Competitive landscape

- **The incumbent at each property** — the real competitor on every renewal, not a named company.
- **Front Range Property Services / Cache La Poudre Landscape** — regional players; the former competes on price at the B/C tier, the latter closer to Ridgeline's positioning but smaller crew capacity.
- **Known limit:** loses on straight price where the board only reads the number — lost a 120-unit HOA in 2025 to a bid 18% under [Dana, 2026-07-28]. Not a pricing mistake to fix; a signal to stop chasing that kind of board.

## 8. Rules that must not be gotten wrong

- **Snow is quoted seasonal-fixed, never per-event.** A light winter is pure profit on a fee already collected. A heavy winter is the cost of a client who stays ten years. Anyone benchmarking Ridgeline against a per-push snow operator is comparing the wrong business entirely.
- **Crew wages are cost of goods; office staff and Dana's comp are overhead.** Ridgeline's gross margin is only a valid comparison against other W-2, self-performing operators — never against broker or subcontractor models, which post 55%+ gross on a completely different cost structure.
- **No subcontracted mowing crews, ever.** This is not a cost decision up for revisiting when margin gets tight — it's the quality-control mechanism the entire recurring-contract model depends on. Proposing subcontracted labor to fix a margin problem misunderstands what's actually being sold.

## 9. Discontinued / pivots

**Residential mowing routes — discontinued March 2024.** Route density never worked in a market this spread out, and every residential stop competed for crew hours against commercial contracts that pay year-round through snow. Not coming back.

**Design-build landscape architecture division — opened 2023, shut down 2024.** Wrong skillset for a crew built around maintenance and quality control, and it pulled Dana's attention away from contract renewals during a season that needed it. Permanent.
